It Starts at Home:
Home Language and Literacy Practices in Jamaica

Shawna-Kaye D. Tucker		                   Victoria A. Murphy, Hamish Chalmers
University of Toronto			                  University of Oxford


Abstract
This study explores the nature of home language and literacy environments (HLLEs) among primary
school learners in Jamaica, contributing to the limited body of Caribbean-based research in this area.
Grounded in ecological systems theory, the study considers how multiple contextual factors in the
home interact to shape children’s literacy development. Data were collected from 114 caregivers who
completed an HLLE questionnaire. Findings indicate generally low levels of print engagement and limited
participation in formal literacy activities (practices known to support reading and writing), with many
caregivers placing greater responsibility on teachers. Jamaican Creole was more frequently used in
many homes than English, potentially influencing children’s transition from conversational fluency
to academic language proficiency in school. At the same time, community religious programs also
emerged as important spaces for supplemental literacy engagement. The study underscores the need
for culturally responsive approaches and tools in literacy research that reflect the broader sociocultural
realities shaping language and literacy development in the Caribbean.

Keywords: home literacy, community literacy, literacy development, Caribbean, writing
